# Elemental Arcana

A magic mod that turns Minecraft into a fantasy adventure, for **NeoForge 1.21.1**. Magic is a power your character has, the way it works in RPGs and isekai: it wakes in you one day and grows as you use it, across eight elements, each with its spells, a skill tree, gear and relics. The world fills with places to find and things to fight (shrines and ley lines, ruins, crypts, mage towers, the Sovereigns' sanctums, sky isles, drake nests, rifts, falling stars, the creatures of the wild), and a voice in a sending stone leads you through it all, to the Hollow. The mod page's text is in [docs/mod-page.md](docs/mod-page.md), and what's in each release in [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md).

## Playing

**Awakening.** You don't pick your element: your magic chooses you. You start with dormant mana and no spells. It wakes by itself, at a small chance each Minecraft day and surely by day 16 (after day 10 the odds rise sharply), or sooner if you survive a brush with an element at 3 hearts or less: fire, drowning, freezing, a hard fall, falling stone or a lightning strike (about a 1 in 3 chance each time, certain from day 10). A brush leans toward its own element, and the **derived elements** are about a hundred times rarer than their kin to wake first: Ice (kin of Water), Crystal (Earth), Lightning (Wind) and Radiance (Fire). Each is reached through its kin's part of the skill tree, and a lightning strike wakes Lightning itself. More elements come from **Catalysts** (Fire, Water, Wind or Earth; 8 of its Essence and a diamond, shapeless): right-click one to try to wake that element. It's used up every try. A second element is very unlikely before level 10 and realistic after; a third, harder one is realistic from level 20, a fourth from level 30 (the odds also rise a little with each level, and each failure helps the next try). There are no element slots; rarity and cost are the limit. Opposed elements (Fire and Radiance vs Water and Ice) can't be combined until level 50. Your own elements hurt you 25% less: their spells, and their everyday damage too (Fire: burning and lava; Ice: freezing; Water: drowning; Wind: falling; Lightning: lightning strikes).

**Gear.** Wands and staves raise Potency and the Affinity of the element they're attuned to (only the better of two held counts): an Apprentice Wand is 2 sticks and an Essence; an Adept Staff (level 15) is an Apprentice Wand, 2 Essence, an amethyst shard and a gold ingot. Mage's robes raise Reservoir, Ward, Focus, Insight and Vitality: apprentice robes are wool and any Essence in the shape of leather armor, adept robes (level 15) an apprentice piece ringed with gold, Essence and an amethyst shard. The master tier (level 35) needs a Guardian Core from a mage tower's Magister: the Master Staff is an Adept Staff, a Guardian Core, 2 diamonds and 2 Essence, all of one element (a Crystal, Lightning or Radiance staff takes its kin's Core: towers hold only the older elements); each master robe piece is its adept piece, a Guardian Core and 2 gold ingots. The archmage tier (level 50) needs a Sovereign Heart: the Archmage Staff (+10 Potency, +6 Focus, +15 Affinity) is a Master Staff, a Heart of its element's family, 2 Essence and 2 netherite scrap; each archmage robe piece (white and gold, the best armour of the robes) is its master piece, a Heart and 2 netherite scrap. Gear adds to the same stats as your stat points and skill tree. Everything is in the mod's creative tab.

**The Arcanist's Journal.** The first time your magic wakes you receive the Arcanist's Journal: how magic works, a chapter for each element you hold, and the old story of the Prime and the Hollow. Lost it? Craft a new one from a book and any Essence.

**Shrines.** Small open-air shrines stand in the wild, each in its element's land: Fire in deserts, badlands and savannas; Water on beaches, rivers and swamps; Ice in snowy lands; Wind on mountains and windswept hills; Earth in plains and forests; and the kin elements' own: Crystal (calcite round amethyst, crystals on its pillars) on stony peaks and shores, Lightning (copper and tuff under lightning rods) on windswept hills and savanna plateaus, Radiance (quartz round a ring of light) in meadows, flower forests and cherry groves. Each has a glowing Shrine Core on a pedestal and an offering chest (Essence, gold, emeralds, amethyst, sometimes a wand or a Catalyst). Within 12 blocks of a core your mana regenerates faster (twice as fast if you hold its element's family). Right-click the core: the first time, you get discovery XP. After that you can use each shrine once a Minecraft day. If your magic still sleeps, it may wake there (1 in 2), leaning toward the shrine's element. If you're awakened, the shrine refills your mana and blesses you for 10 minutes: +5 Affinity of its element family and +2 Potency. **Ley lines** join them: every shrine you touch is remembered, and at any shrine you can sneak and right-click the core with an empty hand to list the others you remember (nearest first, with how far and which way) and click one to travel there, for 10 mana plus 1 per 100 blocks (at most 50); then the lines need a minute to settle.

**Ruins.** Old ruins of weathered stone brick stand all over the overworld's land, about as often as shrines: a Fallen Arch with a cracked altar, a Stone Circle round a sunken floor, or a Ruined Hall with a lectern and old bookshelves. Each hides a chest of old things (bones, iron and gold, emeralds, a little Essence, now and then an apprentice's wand or robe) and often a **page of the old story**: eight written books, from the splitting of the Prime to the last mage who went into the Hollow.

**Arcane Crypts.** Under most land, about one every 600 blocks (never within 400 of the world's centre), a small mausoleum of dark stone marks a crypt: its door opens on a long stair down into the tombs of the first mages, sealed in with runes of their element (the land's, as for mage towers, or now and then its kin). The way runs through an entry hall, a **Hall of Tombs** (upright coffins in the walls burst open as you come near: the land's dead, Attuned to the crypt's element), a **Warded Hall** (glyphs on the floor flare and burst with the element when stepped on: damage and its touch, then rest for 3 seconds; a safe path always runs between the doors, unmarked, and breaking a glyph sets it off) and two **Rune Gates**: a glowing seal nothing breaks, three runestones on pedestals and a keystone over the door that shows how many are lit. Light a rune by casting a spell of its element's family at it, or by using that family's Essence on it (touch it with anything else to learn what it answers to; the crypt's dead often carry its Essence). With all three lit the seal dissolves, and the two coffins beside the gate burst open. Off the way lie a library (old books, now and then one of two pages of the old story) and a storeroom; urns (break them) hold a little everywhere. At the bottom, in the burial chamber, come near the grave flame and the **Revenant** rises from its tomb: the crypt's first warden, a tall skeleton in robes of its element with burning eyes, an Attuned Archmage with its own boss bar. It keeps its distance and casts its element's spells, steps through the grave when struck from close by, raises the chamber's dead at half health and never leaves its chamber (left alone for 20 seconds it goes back to its tomb and heals). When it falls the flame goes out and it leaves its Essence, bones, gold and often a Tome of Insight; the two reliquary chests behind its tomb hold the best of the crypt: diamonds, its Essence, and now and then a Tome of Insight, its family's Catalyst, a Scroll of Unbinding or an Adept Staff of its element.

**Relics.** The first mages were laid to rest with their relics, and a crypt's reliquary chests hold its element's now and then (30% each): the **Ember Heart** (Fire: burning foes take 25% more from your spells, and you don't burn), **Tidecaller's Pearl** (Water: breathe and see underwater, and your Water spells mend you by a tenth of their damage), the **Rimeheart Locket** (Ice: no freezing or slowness, and your spells hit frozen and frosted foes 20% harder), the **Feather of the Gale** (Wind: jump again in mid-air, and no fall damage), the **Stoneheart Idol** (Earth: a hard hit gives you 2 hearts of absorption), the **Prism of the Deep** (Crystal: your Earth and Crystal hits restore mana), the **Storm Sigil** (Lightning: 15% faster, and spells 20% stronger in thunderstorms) and the **Sunstone** (Radiance: you see in the dark and the undead near you smoulder). A Revenant leaves its **Phylactery** one time in four: once every 10 minutes a killing blow leaves you at 1 health instead. Each adds 3 points to a stat. Use a relic to bind it to yourself: its power is yours while it's anywhere in your inventory, and only the one you bound last works.

**Wisps.** Small floating orbs of pure element, one kind per element, drift through their element's lands (the shrine biomes, plus oceans for Water and the Nether for Fire) and guard the shrines, two at each. They leave you alone while your magic sleeps, and they leave their kin alone (mages who hold their element's family). Any other mage is a rival: they circle 5 to 8 blocks away and cast their element's spells. Anyone who hurts one is fought back, and nearby wisps join in. Every wisp is Attuned (a rare one is a Magus or even an Archmage, and bigger), so it's worth Attuned XP, and it drops its element's Essence. Fire wisps fear water and rain. Wisps of the derived elements have no shrines: Crystal wisps drift on stony and jagged peaks, Radiance wisps in meadows, sunflower plains and cherry groves, and Lightning wisps ride thunderstorms (a storm brings twice as many wisps, and half of them are Lightning's). They're the main source of derived Essence (mage tower laboratories and sanctums hold a little). Spawn eggs are in the creative tab.

**Familiars.** A wisp worn down to a quarter of its health can be bound with a **Binding Charm** (an amethyst shard, a Wisp Mote and two Essence): it becomes your familiar, a wisp of its element that floats at your shoulder, follows you (teleporting if left far behind) and fights what you fight with its element's magic, never players, villagers or your other pets. It grows with you: an Adept's spells at first, a Magus's from level 20, an Archmage's from 40. Sneak and right-click it to make it stay or follow; feed it a Wisp Mote to heal it. One familiar at a time: binding another lets the first go. Shrine guardians can't be bound. Your own magic now spares your pets (wolves, cats, horses, your familiar).

**Elemental Golems.** Big, slow guardians of the elements' lands: the **Magma Golem** (Fire), **Tide Golem** (Water), **Gale Golem** (Wind) and **Stone Golem** (Earth), blocky stone bodies with a glowing core. Now and then one stands up 24 to 40 blocks from a player in its element's lands (likelier at night). They ignore the dormant and their own element's kin, but a mage of another element who comes within 12 blocks is a trespasser, and anyone who hurts one is a foe. Within reach it raises both arms (a warning you can step away from) and slams them down: its element's damage all round, and burning ground (Fire), a wave that throws you back and soaks you (Water), a blast that throws you into the air (Wind) or a Tremor along the ground (Earth). They drop their element's Essence and some of their stuff.

**Elemental Drakes.** The elements took wing: the **Fire Drake** (badlands and savanna plateaus), **Frost Drake** (snowy slopes and peaks), **Storm Drake** (windswept hills and stony peaks, and anywhere in a thunderstorm), **Tide Drake** (skimming the sea) and **Gale Drake** (meadows and cherry groves), wyverns with wings 8 blocks across. They circle their homes high up, hunt players they see (not their kin, unless provoked), swoop to rake them and hover to breathe their element in a cone after a glowing warning (fire burns, frost slows and freezes, lightning sparks to another, a torrent shoves and soaks, a gust throws you up), land to rest when they've nothing to hunt, and fight on the ground once badly hurt. Now and then one is born near a player high in its lands (or over the sea), by day, far from the world's centre. **Nests** on the heights hold a drake's **hoard** (gold, emeralds, scales, Essence, now and then its element's relic), and call their drake when someone comes near. Drakes drop **Drake Scales**: four of one element round a gold ingot make a **Drakescale Charm**, which, carried, wards off that element's everyday harm (fire and burning, freezing, lightning, drowning or falls).

**Drake Riding.** Each nest's hoard holds a **Drake Egg** of its drake's element (tide drakes, which don't nest, leave one now and then). Placed and kept warm in its element's way (fire or magma for fire, snow or ice for frost, copper or a lightning rod for storm, twice as fast in a thunderstorm, water for tide, the open sky above height 120 for gale), it cracks and hatches after 20 minutes of warmth into a hatchling that's yours if you're near: it follows you, sits when told (sneak and use it) and fights beside you. It grows into a juvenile after a day and an adult after three, faster fed its element's Essence or meat. Saddle a grown one (a **Drake Saddle**: a saddle, four Drake Scales and two gold ingots) and ride it: it walks on the ground, takes off when you jump, flies where you look (forward faster, back to a hover, jump to climb), breathes its element when you press the cast key, and tires: a stamina bar shows over the hotbar, and spent, it glides down to land and rest.

**Creatures of the Wild.** Each kind of land has creatures of its own. In old forests a **Thornwood Treant** stands among the trees as one of them (oak, birch, spruce or dark oak, as its forest grows), still and silent, until someone who isn't of the Earth family comes within 6 blocks or hits it: then it uproots, lumbers after them, sweeps its branch-arms and stamps to burst roots up under its target (**Rooted**: no walking or jumping for 2 seconds). Fire burns it badly; it heals in the sun, and with no one to chase it walks home and takes root again. Over the snows at night drift **Frost Wraiths**, hooded and half seen: they circle their prey casting icicles, and once it's frosted through, swoop in and freeze it with a touch; hit, they flicker a few blocks away, and daylight burns them off. In the badlands, deserts and the Nether, **Ember Salamanders** bask, minding their own business unless someone comes within 4 blocks: then they spit fire and bite (setting you alight); they swim and heal in lava, and water hurts them. Over the windswept peaks by day, **Gale Harpies** circle: one dives screeching, snatches you in its talons, climbs six blocks and lets go (hurt it and it lets go sooner), or hangs in the air and beats its wings at you. Deep in the caves, **Crystal Crawlers** climb the walls with glowing crystals on their backs, fling fans of crystal shards and burst when they die. In swamps, **Bog Lurkers** lie in the water with only their eyes showing: step to the edge and one pounces, bites and drags you under, or spits mud from the water (slowness, a moment's blindness). These three leave **Harpy Plumes**, **Prism Cores** and **Bog Pearls**. They leave **Heartwood**, **Wraith Silk** and **Salamander Scales** now and then, which make **Trophies of the Wild**, charms that teach their creature's trick, carried anywhere in your inventory: the **Heartwood Talisman** (two Heartwood, an Earth Essence and a string) heals you resting in the sun on the earth and keeps roots off you; the **Wraithsilk Veil** (two Wraith Silk, an Ice Essence and a phantom membrane) halves a hit and flickers you 4 to 6 blocks away from it, every 12 seconds at most; the **Salamander Charm** (two Salamander Scales, a Fire Essence and a gold nugget) cools still lava under your feet into a crust you can walk on (it melts back behind you), and hot ground doesn't burn you. The second three make three more: the **Plume of the Gale** (two Harpy Plumes, a Wind Essence and a feather) carries a sprinting jump twice as far and wards off falls of up to 8 blocks; the **Crawler's Prism** (two Prism Cores, a Crystal Essence and an amethyst shard) lets you climb any wall you walk into (sneak to hold still); the **Bog Pearl Charm** (two Bog Pearls, a Water Essence and a slimeball) lets you breathe under water and swim half again as fast.

**Arcane Infusion.** An **Infusion Altar** (amethyst shards, gold ingots, obsidian, smooth stone and any Essence) binds an element into a weapon or armour piece: set it on the altar and pour in eight Essence of one element. An infused sword, axe, mace, trident, bow or crossbow strikes with its element: the element chart applies, the element lands (fire burns, water soaks, ice frosts, wind gusts, earth staggers, lightning sparks to one more foe, radiance sears the undead, crystal now and then shatters for more) and the reactions follow (Melt, Vaporize, Freeze, Swirl, Crystallize). Each infused armour piece (robes too) wards off a tenth of its element family's harm, and four of one element add a boon: fire can't burn you, you breathe under water, frost can't take you (and chills your attackers), falls can't hurt you, you can't be knocked back, you're quicker, the sun heals you, or a crystal ward renews itself.

**Cantrips.** Not all magic is for fighting. **Cantrip Scrolls**, found in the chests of crypt and tower libraries, sanctums, ruins, Arcanist cottages, mage-tower laboratories and drake hoards (and sold by Arcanists), teach any awakened mage a cantrip of the **Arcane** school for good: **Mage Light** (a floating orb of light where you look, until you punch it out), **Prospect** (for 8 seconds, see the ores within 12 blocks outlined through the stone), **Recall** (stand still for 4 seconds to be taken to your bed or respawn anchor, without using its charge; moving or being hurt breaks it and gives the cooldown back), **Mend** (a fifth of a worn tool's durability back), **Water Breathing**, **Featherfall** and **Night Eye**. Learned cantrips join the spell wheel, which widens to fit them.

**Sky Isles.** Far apart (about one every 750 blocks), islands float 70 to 100 blocks over the land or sea: grassy tops with a few trees and flowers over an upside-down mountain of earth and stone (ores and amethyst in its rock, roots hanging from its point), and at the heart of each the ruin of a pavilion, calcite pillars round a mosaic floor, with a chest of sky loot (Wind Essence, feathers, golden food, emeralds, now and then the Feather of the Gale or an elytra, and a Cantrip Scroll one time in four). Wind wisps keep them; one in two has a smaller islet nearby. Reach them by gliding or on a drake.

**Starfall.** Some nights (one in three for each awakened mage) a star falls 100 to 180 blocks away: a streak across the sky, a boom, and a message saying which way. Where it lands, the ground is scorched, Radiance wisps gather, and a pale gold pillar of light stands over the **Fallen Star** until dawn (then it cools into Starstone). Mine it for **Star Fragments**: four with an iron ingot and a glass pane make a **Starlit Lantern** (light 15, and no hostile monster spawns within 24 blocks), two with a Radiance Essence a **Wishing Star** (mana, health and every cooldown restored, once). Arcanists buy fragments too.

**Charm Pouch.** Charms and relics work from anywhere in the inventory, and soon they fill it. A **Charm Pouch** (five leather, a string and a gold ingot) holds nine of them, the Drakescale Charms, the Trophies of the Wild and the relics (the item tag `elementalarcana:charms`), and they all keep working from inside it: the wards, the tricks, and the relic you bound last. Use it to open it (shift-click charms in and out), or right-click a charm onto it, or it onto a charm, as with a bundle; its tooltip shows what's inside.

**Seeker's Compass.** A compass, two amethyst shards and any Essence make a compass that finds things. Sneak and use it to choose what it seeks (shrines, ruins, crypts, mage towers, sanctums, drake nests or sky isles); use it, and its needle swings toward the nearest, however far, with word of which way and how far. It points there until you arrive, then settles.

**The Voice in the Stone.** When your magic wakes you're given a **Sending Stone**, and someone speaks through it: Caelith, the last Keeper, who carried the Prime Key into the Hollow long ago and never came out. Use the stone to listen. Fourteen chapters lead from your first shrine, through the wisps, the Arcanists, the crypts, the towers and the Sovereigns' sanctums, to the Hollow itself, telling the old story as you go; each chapter's task is one of the mod's advancements, and each is worth XP and something for the road ahead (a Seeker's Compass, a Binding Charm, a Charm Pouch...). The stone grows warm when a task is done. Lost it? A smooth stone, an amethyst shard and any Essence make another.

**Wandering Mages.** Now and then (a morning in three) a wandering mage comes to an awakened player, in a midnight-blue robe and a wide hat; you sense it, and which way. It stays two days, hides at night as the wandering trader does, and trades: three **rumours** (for emeralds and a compass: maps with a red X on the nearest crypt, mage tower, sky isle, drake nest or ruin), four wares from far away (Cantrip Scrolls, Wishing Stars, Star Fragments, rare Essence, a Seeker's Compass, a Charm Pouch...), and it buys Wisp Motes and Star Fragments.

**Wisp Rings.** Rare rings of mushrooms and flowers on a band of dark podzol, round a mossy stone, grow in forests, meadows and plains (about one every 600 blocks). By night the wisps dance over them (seven little lights, the elements' colours) and they chime softly. Step into one at night and the wisps do something to you, once a night: two times in three a boon (Fey Luck, Wisp Sight, Moonlit Step, a gift, your mana filled), otherwise a trick (turned around and set down far off, shrunk to half your size for two minutes, or three cross wisps to fight).

**Arcane Flora.** Eight herbs, one for each element, grow where their element runs strong: Emberblooms in deserts, badlands, savannas and the Nether, Moonlilies on swamp and river water (open and glowing at night), Skyplumes on windswept hills, Deepcaps on cave floors, Frostcaps in the snow, Prismleaves on stony peaks and in dripstone caves, Stormthistles on windswept savannas, Sunpetals in meadows (open by day). Most glow a little and give off a little of their element. Brew one into an Awkward Potion for a tonic of Kinship with its element: its spells 20% stronger and its magic 20% softer on you for three minutes (longer with redstone, 35% with glowstone). Herbs go in flower pots, bone meal gives a second, and Arcanists buy them and sell tonics.

**The Hollowed.** Some heard the Hollow's hunger when the seals faded, and let it in: they make no mana now, they eat yours, and what they eat heals them. Hollowed Acolytes keep their distance and throw Hunger Bolts, Devourers rush in with mana-eating claws, and a Hollow Herald (a purple bar) leads each camp: it throws bolts three at once, drags everyone in with the Pull, and its hunger eats your mana just for standing near. Their camps (black tents round a Hunger Obelisk that keeps them strong, about one every 600 blocks in plains, forests, taiga, savannas and meadows) hold a chest; break the obelisk to weaken them. At dusk they may hunt a mage of level 15 or more. Their Hollow Shards make a Hungerward Charm, which halves what they, and the Hollow itself, eat. Caelith has a chapter on them.

**Wonders of the Wild.** Gentle creatures that fight no one. At night glowmoths, their wings glowing in an element's colour, come to the herbs and flowers and circle any light; catch one in a glass bottle and set it down as a Moth Jar, a lantern that never goes out. High over the sea, skyrays glide in slow wide circles, five blocks from wingtip to wingtip, their spots glowing at night; glide near one (with elytra or Skyward Leap) and its wake bears you up, so you can follow them across the ocean.

**Ley Anchors.** Make your own place on the ley lines: stone bricks, amethyst and an ender pearl make a Ley Anchor. Name it in an anvil, set it down at home and touch it; then travel to it from any shrine, or from it to any shrine or anchor you remember, as the shrines' ley lines run. Friends who touch it can use it too. The Seeker's Compass now also finds Hollowed camps and wisp rings, and Wandering Mages sell rumours of the camps.

**The Circle.** The mages who keep the old oaths gather in rare Enclaves (about one every 1,500 blocks in plains, meadows and forests): a walled courtyard round a white Spire with a library, the Archmagister's study and an observatory under a glass dome, an herb garden of all eight herbs, a duelling ring, and two Arcanists' cottages. Four Circle Mages of four elements keep it, greet you with a word that follows your story, and fight any monster that comes near (and the Hollowed above all; a mage of water even mends your wounds mid-fight); your spells fly through them and theirs through you. One who falls is replaced the next day. The Seeker's Compass finds Enclaves, and Wandering Mages sell rumours of them.

**The Circle's work.** Use the Archmagister and, once your magic is awake, you're given a **Circle Commission**: a deed against the Hollow's servants or the world's great foes, harder as your story goes on (slay the Hollowed or close a rift as a Novice; break a Hunger Obelisk or slay a Magus as an Adept; a tower's Magister or a crypt's Revenant as a Master; a Sovereign as an Archmage). It counts as you go, like a bounty; bring it back finished for **Marks of the Circle** and experience. Sneak and use the Archmagister to spend them at the Circle's stores: Tomes of Insight, Scrolls of Unbinding, the Hungerward Charm, brews, Wisp Motes, rumours of crypts, sanctums and sky isles, and the **Sigil of the Circle**, which calls a mage of the Circle to fight at your side for two minutes.

**Duels.** Hold out a Mark of the Circle to one of an Enclave's mages and they'll take it as a wager and meet you in the Enclave's duelling ring. Step in, a bell and a count of three, and you fight, your spells and theirs fair game for each other for the length of the bout. No one dies of a duel: a blow that would bring either of you below a fifth of your health stops there, and you yield. Step out of the ring and you yield too; after two minutes it's a draw. Win, and your mark comes back with two more; win three duels, and the Archmagister will face you (for five). Each mage fights one bout a day.

**Cinder Forges.** In the Nether's wastes, over the lava sea, the first mages' smithies still stand, ruined, carved deep into the netherrack: a round hall of blackstone under a vaulted roof, chains and soul lanterns, a ring of lava round an anvil on a dais, the forge's tools, and two storerooms behind iron bars. Come near and the **Forgewarden** wakes, a construct of blackstone and magma nearly twice a golem's size: it slams the floor, hurls magma at those who keep their distance, vents fire all round it, and at half its strength turns molten, faster and leaving burning ground. Fire and lava don't touch it; water and ice hurt it most. It leaves an **Ember Core**, which makes a **Forgefire Charm** (fire and lava harm you a third less, and you burn out twice as fast) and burns in a furnace as long as a bucket of lava. The Seeker's Compass finds the forges, in the Nether. The Charm Pouch now also takes the Hungerward Charm.

**Tempering.** Every Cinder Forge's dais holds an **Ember Anvil** (and you can make one: an anvil, an Ember Core, two polished blackstone and three magma blocks). Use it holding a wand, a staff or a piece of a mage's robe, with an Ember Core and five levels of experience to spend, and the piece is tempered: each of its stats grows by one, up to three tempers (an Adept Staff of Fire tempered three times gives Potency +7, Focus +5, Fire Affinity +9). The forges' storerooms hold a spare Ember Core now and then.

**Creatures of the Nether.** **Ash Wraiths**, kin of the frost wraiths in ash-grey and soul fire, drift over the soul sand valleys: they circle you casting fire spells, and once you burn, their touch withers you; water and ice tear them. **Cinder Hounds** hunt the hot wastes in packs of two to four: they run you down and leap, their bite sets you alight, and hurting one brings the pack; water and rain hurt them. Their trophies: the **Ashen Shroud** (Soul Ash round a Phantom Membrane; carried, the undead don't notice you unless you strike them first) and the **Houndstooth Charm** (Cinder Fangs and gold on a chain; carried, you run a sixth faster in the Nether).

**Arcanists.** A villager profession for magic, in purple robes and a wide-brimmed hat. Its workstation is the **Arcane Lectern** (a lectern, an amethyst shard and two Essence, shapeless). Any unemployed villager near one takes the job, and about one village in three already has an Arcanist's cottage. Arcanists buy Essence and amethyst. They sell the Journal, wands and staves, robes, Essence of any element and Catalysts. Only they sell two rarities: the **Scroll of Unbinding** (Expert), which gives back every skill tree node you bought for free, and the **Tome of Insight** (Master), worth one bonus tree point.

**Bounties.** Arcanists also post bounties: among their trades, a **Bounty Contract** for an emerald. Each asks one thing, harder at higher trade levels: slay creatures Attuned to an element (5 to 8), defeat wisps (3 to 5), slay a Magus, close a rift, or slay an Archmage. Carry it and it counts what you do (its tooltip shows how far along it is, and it glints once it's done); right-click any Arcane Lectern with a finished one for its reward: emeralds and experience, with Essence, Wisp Motes, a Tome of Insight, a Catalyst or a Scroll of Unbinding depending on the task.

**Mage towers.** Rare towers of elemental stone, about one every 900 blocks and never within 800 blocks of the world's centre: a round tower 13 blocks across and about 40 tall, its element from the land around it. Five floors (a hall, a library, a laboratory, an armory and the sanctum) each have loot. The first time someone comes near, the tower wakes and acolytes appear on every floor: robed casters of its element who keep their distance and fight anyone who isn't kin. Reach the sanctum and its **Magister** appears: an Archmage with a boss bar who blinks away when hurt and calls wisps at half health. When it falls, the Tower Heart goes dark and it leaves a **Guardian Core**, the key to master gear.

**Sanctums and Sovereigns.** Far out in the world (never within 1500 blocks of its centre) stand the four sanctums, open-air arenas of elemental stone: the Caldera of Fire (deserts, badlands and savannas, with lava pits), the Tidehall of Water (coasts and swamps, sometimes rising out of the shallows, with pools and channels), the Aerie of Wind (mountains and windswept hills, open to the sky) and the Deepvault of Earth (forests, taiga and plains, walled and crowned with amethyst). Each holds a Seal; come within 10 blocks and its Sovereign rises: **Vulkhar** of Flame, **Thalassa** of Tides, **Caelum** of Storms or **Orvald** of Stone, an Archmage of level 50 or more with its own boss bar. It hovers over you casting its element's spells and two of its own (Rain of Embers and Inferno; Tidal Surge and Drowning Bubble; Gale Volley and Tempest; Quake and Bulwark, whose guardian wisps make it nearly untouchable while they live), and at half health it calls two wisps and burns brighter. Kin are tested like anyone else. If no one fights it for 20 seconds it drifts back to its seal and heals. When it falls the seal is restored for good and it leaves a **Sovereign Heart**, the key to archmage gear and to the way to the Hollow. Master Arcanists sell **Sanctum Maps** (24 emeralds and a compass).

**Elemental Rifts.** Once your magic has woken, the world sometimes tears open near you: most often at night (about one night in four), more in storms, now and then by day, 24 to 40 blocks away on open ground, never in villages, towers, shrines or sanctums. You're told ("The air tears open nearby: a rift of Fire!"), and a pillar of the element's light marks the spot. A rift is a jagged tear in the air in its element's colour, and it sends out three waves of creatures Attuned to its element, the land's own (zombies, skeletons and spiders; husks in the desert, strays in the snow): three Adepts and a wisp, then three Adepts, a Magus and a wisp, then the **Rift Warden** with its guard (an Archmage, or a Magus while you're below level 15). A boss bar shows the wave. When the last wave is down the rift collapses with a flash and throws out its cache: its element's Essence, experience, emeralds, gold, amethyst and the like, and often a Tome of Insight, a Scroll of Unbinding or a Catalyst. Only kills count: left alone for a minute, open for five, or cheated (the world turned peaceful), it fades and pulls its creatures back in.

**Auras and mana weather.** Look at a creature and you sense its aura, as much as your Insight reads: below 5 only how dangerous it feels (a mark under the crosshair, green for weak, gold for even, red for strong, violet for deadly), from 5 its level, from 15 its element too. A hostile creature 15 or more levels above you within 12 blocks puts **Pressure** on you: each rank costs 12% of your spell power and 20% of your mana regeneration, and every 4 Ward counts as a level less of gap. **H** hides your own aura: creatures notice you at half the distance, but your mana returns at half speed. Mana regeneration also depends on the air: richer in high-level lands (up to +40%), a quarter in the deep dark (and in the Hollow), a quarter faster in your elements' lands and a fifth slower in their opposites'. The mana bar shows the multiplier when the air isn't ordinary. Every 4 to 8 days a **mana tide** rises at nightfall and runs until dawn: mana flows half again as fast, awakening, Attunement and wisps come twice as often, creatures are born 5 levels stronger, and the sky turns violet.

**The Hollow.** The four Sovereign Hearts (one of each element) around an eye of ender, amethyst in the corners, make the **Prime Key**. Hold it for two seconds in the Overworld and the way opens: you, and everyone within 6 blocks, are taken to **the Hollow**, a void under a dead sky where the Ruin hangs: an island with the four restored seals on broken pillars and a maw at its heart. Three seconds later the **Hollow** rises, level 70 with about a thousand health, wearing the elements it devoured. It fights in four forms, a quarter of its health each (Fire, Water, Wind, then Earth), Attuned to each in turn, so strike every shape with what beats it. It casts that element's spells and its Sovereign's, and every change of form is a Devouring that drags everyone in and throws them back out. While it lives, its hunger eats 5 mana a second from everyone near, and health once the mana is gone. Bind it and everyone there gets a **Heart of the Prime**; ten seconds later the Hollow lets them go home. Used, the Heart of the Prime awakens every element you don't hold yet, opposites included, and gives 5 tree points. (The Hollow is a dimension of the mod's own, so the first time a world loads with it Minecraft warns about experimental settings once; that's expected.)

**Brews.** Brewed from an Awkward Potion like any potion (and made splash, lingering or into arrows the same way): a **Mana Draught** (amethyst shard) refills 30% of your mana at once (60% with glowstone); an **Elixir of Clarity** (a Wisp Mote, which wisps drop) makes mana regenerate twice as fast for 3 minutes; an **Elixir of Focus** (lapis lazuli) gives +6 Focus and an **Elixir of Warding** (prismarine crystals) +6 Ward. Glowstone makes the elixirs stronger and redstone longer. Arcanists sell Mana Draughts and Elixirs of Clarity.

**The path.** An *Elemental Arcana* tab in the advancements screen (L) maps the adventure, from waking your magic through shrines, Arcanists, mage towers and sanctums to the Prime Key and the Hollow, with the side roads (reactions, keystones, a spell's capstone, gear) along the way. Each step says what to do next.

**Companion mods** (all optional; the mod needs none of them): [JEI](https://modrinth.com/mod/jei) lists each element's wands, staves, Guardian Cores and Sovereign Hearts on their own, with their recipes, and gives the things found in the world (Essence, Catalysts, Wisp Motes, Cores, Hearts, the Prime Key, the Arcanist's goods) a page saying where they come from and what they do. [Jade](https://modrinth.com/mod/jade) shows creatures' levels and elements. [Veil](https://modrinth.com/mod/veil) gives glowing spell effects real bloom. LambDynamicLights or Sodium Dynamic Lights make spells light up their surroundings.

**Level and stats**
- Everyone has a level, up to 100: you, and every creature in the world. Each level needs 9% more XP than the last.
- XP is mana you absorb from what you kill: more from higher-level creatures, Attuned ones and big ones, less from weaker ones, and nothing from creatures 10 or more levels below you. Setting off a reaction (Melt, Vaporize, Freeze, Swirl) on a creature gives a fifth of its kill XP, once every 5 seconds. Casting itself gives no XP.
- The level gap matters in every fight: each level of difference makes the stronger side hit 4.5% harder and take 4.5% less, both ways (a creature 20 levels above you hits about 2.4 times as hard and takes 2.4 times less).
- Each level gives a **stat point**. Spend them in the **Stats** window (the Status window's top-left button):

  | Stat | Does |
  |---|---|
  | Reservoir | Max mana and regen (both also grow a little with your level) |
  | Potency | Power of every spell |
  | Affinity (one per element family; Ice belongs to Water's, Crystal to Earth's, Lightning to Wind's, Radiance to Fire's) | Power of that family's spells |
  | Focus | Shorter cooldowns |
  | Ward | Less elemental damage taken |
  | Vitality | More max health |
  | Insight | Harder reaction hits, more Essence |

  Points are worth the most up to 20 in a stat, less up to 40 and less again after that.
- Mana regenerates over time. To meditate, sneak and stand still for 2 seconds; regen is tripled while you do. A full night's sleep refills your mana.
- Emptying your mana gives you *Mana Sickness* for 15s: slower movement, weaker attacks and half regen.
- If you cast without enough mana, the missing amount is paid in health (1 heart per 20 mana). This is called overcasting. It never kills you and always causes Mana Sickness. The HUD mana bar pulses red when your next cast would overcast.

**Conjuring** (Icicle, Fireball, Wind Blade): press R to conjure one projectile at a time, up to your spell level's maximum. Each costs mana when conjured (the first full price, each extra less), grows while you hold it, and costs 1 mana per second to keep; if you can't pay, everything you hold launches itself. Left click launches one, right click launches all. The cooldown starts when the last one leaves. With a full, fully grown set, hold R for a second to fuse it (Glacial Lance, Sunfire, Storm Scythe).

**Bubble Prison** (Water, level 3): tap R to trap the creature (or player) under your crosshair in a floating water bubble for 4 seconds. It's helpless and Wet, which sets up Freeze and Vaporize combos, and any hit pops it for +4 damage. Bosses can't be trapped.

**Frost Shield** (Ice, level 3): tap R to raise orbiting ice shards that absorb damage (shown as ice hearts) and shatter outward when broken.

**Fire** has **Melt**: a fire hit on a frozen or frosted enemy thaws it in a burst of steam for 75% more damage.

**Water** makes enemies **Wet** (so does standing in rain or water). **Vaporize**: water on a burning enemy, or fire on a wet one, deals 50% more damage in a burst of steam. **Freeze**: ice on a wet enemy, or water on a frosted one, freezes it solid.

**Elements matter.** Every spell deals damage of its element, and some creatures are born with an element of their own. Water is strong against Fire, Fire against Ice and Ice against Water (×1.5); Earth is strong against Wind and Water is strong against Earth (×1.5); Lightning runs through Water, Earth and Crystal ground Lightning, Crystal is strong against Wind, and Radiance hits like Fire (strong against Ice, weak against Water); and every element resists itself (×0.5). Everything else is neutral. The game never tells you a creature's element: watch and listen to how your hits land. (With [Jade](https://modrinth.com/mod/jade) installed, its tooltip shows the element.)

**Creature magic.** Once someone has awakened magic, hostile mobs nearby occasionally spawn **Attuned** to an element: an **Adept** (5%), a **Magus** (1%, 8 levels above the creatures around it) or, very rarely, an **Archmage** (0.1%, 20 levels above them, with a boss bar). Every creature's level comes from where it is: 1 to 5 near world spawn and higher further away (up to +30), more in deep caves, structures and the deep dark; the Nether is level 25 to 50 and the End 60 and up. Creatures put their stat points into Vitality, Ward and Potency. They are more common far from world spawn (up to three times as likely), and their element leans toward the biome's. Attuned creatures are hit by the element chart like any elemental creature, and they cast their element's spells: Adepts know one, Magi two, Archmages three (Fire: fireballs, burning ground, Meteors; Water: a jet, healing their allies, a whirlpool; Ice: icicles, Frost Nova, an ice ward; Wind: wind blades, Gale Dash, Updraft; Earth: boulders, a Tremor, a stone ward). Every cast is announced by half a second of glowing hands and a warning sound: move! Getting close doesn't stop them: up close they cast point-blank, or burst their element in your face to shove you back. Defeating one gives far more XP than an ordinary creature (Adept ×3, Magus ×5, Archmage ×12) and may drop **Elemental Essence** of its element (Adept: half the time; Magus: 1-2; Archmage: 3-5). Creatures born with an element, like blazes, drop it now and then too. Essence feeds your spells (see below). Watch for a faint hint of their element.

**Earth** is the heavy, sturdy element. Its signature is **Crystallize**: an Earth hit on a burning, wet or frozen creature puts that out and leaves a shard where it stood; walk into the shard for 3 hearts of absorption for 10 seconds (a creature leaves one shard every 5 seconds). Boulder grows into Landslide or Bedrock at Lv 5 and Mountainfall or Avalanche at Lv 10; Stone Skin into Bastion or Crystal Carapace and Fortress or Titan. Earth's brush (to wake it first) is surviving a falling block, stalagmite, anvil or suffocation at 3 hearts or less. Innate Earth creatures: iron golems, silverfish and armadillos.

**Wind** has two signature mechanics. **Swirl**: a wind hit on a frozen, frosted, burning or wet enemy spreads that element to every creature within 4 blocks. **Airborne**: enemies launched by wind take 25% more damage until they land.

**The skill tree.** Each level also gives a **tree point** for the skill tree (the Status window's **Tree** button, or click a spell there). It's one big map: a ring of small stat nodes in the middle and a region for each element around it (Wind north, Fire east, Water south, Earth west; Ice is a cluster on Water's outer edge, behind Water Affinity 10, and Crystal, Lightning and Radiance are clusters off Earth, Wind and Fire, behind their kin's Affinity 10). Your element's start node is free and gives you its first spell (Fireball, Hydro Jet, Icicle, Wind Blade, Boulder, Prism Bolt, Chain Lightning or Smite). From there you take nodes next to ones you hold: small nodes add +2 to a stat, spell nodes unlock the other spells, and each leveled spell (Fireball, Hydro Jet, Icicle, Frost Shield, Wind Blade, Chain Lightning and others) has a chain of nodes behind it, its levels 2 to 10, splitting into a choice of paths at Lv 5 and 10 (taking one locks the other). A spell's next level also needs its mastery bar full: casting fills it, and so does **Infusing** Essence of its element (from the tree's bottom panel). Each level also shortens the spell's cooldown (Lv 10: under half of Lv 1), and so does Focus. Regions of elements you don't have are sealed, but kin (Water and Ice, Earth and Crystal, Wind and Lightning, Fire and Radiance) reach into each other's. Right-click a node you hold to give it back, or right-click a stat in the Stats window, for 1 Essence (plus 1 every 20 levels), as long as the rest of your tree stays connected. **Condense** Essence into bonus tree points from the Status screen (8 Essence for the first, 4 more for each after).

**Notables and keystones.** Each region ends one arm in a **notable** (a gold-ringed node with several stats at once, like Kindled Soul: +6 Fire Affinity, +4 Potency) and a **keystone** (a big purple node that changes a rule of magic, for better and worse): **Glass Cannon** (Fire: spells hit 30% harder, 30% less health), **Wellspring** (Water: mana regenerates 60% faster, 25% less mana), **Gale Step** (Wind: 15% faster and no fall damage, but every element hurts 15% more), **Mountain's Heart** (Earth: 40% more health and no knockback, 15% slower), **Winter's Grasp** (Ice: spells hit the frozen and frosted 30% harder, fire hurts 40% more) and, out past the core ring, **Blood Magic** (spells cost health instead of mana, a heart per 20, and never cause Mana Sickness). The derived clusters each end in a notable, **Prismatic Ward** (Crystal: +6 Ward, +4 Earth Affinity), **Static Charge** (Lightning: +6 Potency, +4 Wind Affinity) and **Inner Light** (Radiance: +6 Vitality, +4 Fire Affinity), and then a keystone: **Refraction** (Crystal: every third projectile that would hit you turns back at its shooter, melee blows hurt 20% more), **Conductor** (Lightning: your spell hits arc on to the nearest other foe within 5 blocks for 35% of the damage, 20% less mana) and **Sunborn** (Radiance: in bright light, or a Dawnbreak's, your spells hit 25% harder and you mend; in dim light they hit 20% weaker).

Operators can use `/arcana level set <n>`, `/arcana xp add <n>`, `/arcana affinity add <element>`, `/arcana affinity reset`, `/arcana awaken <element>`, `/arcana awaken day <n>`, `/arcana awaken clock`, `/arcana mana fill|set <n>`, `/arcana attune <targets> <element> <rank>|none`, `/arcana cooldowns reset`, `/arcana stats reset`, `/arcana stats spend <count> <stat>` (e.g. `potency` or `affinity/fire`) and `/arcana creaturelevel <targets> [set <n>]`, `/arcana tree info|take <node>|refund <node>|reset`, `/arcana spell <spell> <level> [branches]` (a spell at that level through its tree path, free, with the named branches at its forks, e.g. `/arcana spell elementalarcana:updraft 10 skyfall endless_sky`), `/arcana wisp spawn [element]` (calls a wisp near you now) `/arcana sanctum reset` (seals again the sanctums within 3 chunks, so their Sovereigns can be fought again) and `/arcana tide start|stop` (a mana tide now, or no more).

**Server settings.** The mod's server settings are in `config/elementalarcana-server.toml` (a copy in a world's `serverconfig/` folder overrides them for that world; in single player, also on the Mods screen's config button): how often **rifts**, **starfall**, **wisps**, the **Creatures of the Wild**, wild **drakes**, **golems** and **Wandering Mages** come, as multiples of their usual chances (0 turns one off), whether **mana tides** rise, how quickly magic wakes **naturally** (0: only a brush with an element, a Catalyst or a command), and whether the **Sending Stone** is given. Commands that call these things up ignore them; `/arcana config` shows the settings in force.

## Adding a spell

The built-in spells are registered this same way, so anything they do, yours can do too.

1. **Write it.** Extend `Spell`. If it fires a projectile, also implement `ProjectileSpell`; it will reuse the shared `SpellProjectile` entity, so you need no new entity or renderer.

   ```java
   public class LightningSpell extends Spell {
       public LightningSpell() {
           // school, mana cost, cooldown in ticks, required magic level
           super(MySchools.STORM, 30, 60, 8);
       }

       @Override
       public CastResult cast(CastContext context) {
           // context.caster(), context.level(), context.look(), context.power() ...
           return CastResult.SUCCESS; // or CastResult.fail(Component) to cancel at no cost
       }
   }
   ```

2. **Register it:**

   ```java
   DeferredRegister<Spell> SPELLS = DeferredRegister.create(SpellRegistries.SPELL_KEY, "yourmod");
   SPELLS.register("lightning", LightningSpell::new);
   ```

3. **Add assets:** lang keys `spell.yourmod.lightning` and `spell.yourmod.lightning.desc`, plus a 16×16 icon at `assets/yourmod/textures/spell/lightning.png`.

That's all it takes. Players who have awakened the spell's element and reached its level will see it in the wheel, Status window and HUD, with mana, cooldowns, XP and syncing handled for them.

**New element:** register a `SpellSchool` (color + cast sound) on `SpellRegistries.SCHOOL_KEY` and add the lang keys `school.yourmod.<name>` and `school.yourmod.<name>.desc`. It joins the awakening roll once it has a region in the skill tree (the roll currently covers the built-in elements).

**Shields:** implement `ShieldSpell` and raise a `SpellShield` in `cast`. Absorbing damage, ice hearts, orbiting shards and syncing are handled for you, and the hooks cover parries, reflections, breaking and death-saves.

**Multi-hit spells:** deal damage with `SpellDamage.hurtMultiHit(...)`. Minecraft otherwise ignores hits that land within half a second of each other, so a volley would only count once.

**Hooks:** `SpellCastEvent.Pre` (cancel a cast or change its mana cost) and `SpellCastEvent.Post`, both on `NeoForge.EVENT_BUS`.
